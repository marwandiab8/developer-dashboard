For Ashley

of you.

Not just because you got the job. Because you finally started seeing in yourself what I’ve been seeing in you all along.

What I see in you
The job didn’t suddenly make you more capable. It just gave the rest of the world a chance to catch up.
Your mind

You see things other people miss.

One of the things I admire most about you is the way your mind works.

You notice details. You think three steps ahead. You see the practical solution before most people have even finished looking at the problem.

The truckI can look at everything we need to fit in the back and say, “There’s no way.” You look at it for a second and say, “Yes there is. Do it this way.” And somehow, you’re right.
The patio stonesI’m thinking about how to level the stone in front of me. You’re already thinking about the step we might have missed and the problem it will create later.
Everyday lifeYou are observant, organized and practical in a way that constantly surprises me. You don’t just see a problem. You see the whole system around it.
And yes, sometimes it almost offends me how quickly you see the answer—because I’m supposed to be the practical one.

But underneath the joke, what I really feel is admiration. I have spent most of my life solving problems, building things and figuring out how pieces fit together. So when someone can make me stop and think, “Damn, she saw that before I did,” I notice.

You do that to me all the time.

The part I want you to remember

Ashley, this job is not a fluke.

For a long time, you thought your experience meant you should be somewhere around $45,000 or $50,000 a year. I never believed that was the limit of what you were worth.

I saw something different.

I saw your intelligence. Your life experience. Your judgment. Your ability to organize things, notice what everybody else misses, and solve real problems in a way that actually works.

I believed you were capable of much more. But the part that makes me the proudest is not that I believed it.

It’s that you finally believed it too.

You went out there and earned a position paying $75,000, with pension and benefits, after being away from work for three years. You walked into that opportunity carrying all the doubts you had about what you were “qualified” to do—and you convinced yourself that your experience, your intelligence and everything you have learned through life actually count.

That takes courage.

I hope when you look at this job, you don’t only see a salary or a position. I hope you see proof. Proof that you were always more capable than the number you had put on yourself.

When I got my job, you wrote me something that made me feel seen. You told me how proud you were of me, and it meant a lot to me.

This is me telling you the same thing.

I see you.

I see how smart you are.

I see how capable you are.

I see the way your brain works.

And I see how much more there still is ahead of you.

I’m not proud of you only because you got the job. I’m proud of you because you finally gave yourself permission to go after something worthy of you.

And now that you’ve done it once, I hope you never put that old ceiling over yourself again.

You earned this.

And I am incredibly proud of you.

Love,
Marwan ♥
